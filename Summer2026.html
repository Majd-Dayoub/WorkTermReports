01

Introduction

From May to August 2026, I returned to Co-operators Insurance for my second four-month term, completing an eight-month placement that began in Winter 2026. Working from the Guelph office, I continued contributing to the audit automation project, with a stronger focus on production environments, OpenShift, and DevOps.

This report reflects on my transition from building the application's logic and website to preparing the environments that support it. It also covers how technical troubleshooting, Jira administration, and presentations helped me grow beyond software development alone.

02

About the employer

Co-operators is a Canadian insurance and financial services organization. My work connected to the computing side of the organization through cloud engineering, internal software, and automation that supports audit workflows.

I continued working from the Guelph office, a beautiful space that made it an enjoyable place to spend my work term. Returning to the same employer allowed me to build on the project knowledge and working relationships I developed during the winter.

03

Goals & learning outcomes

Critical & creative thinking ? Problem solving

Goal: Improve my ability to independently solve complex cloud and software problems.

Action plan: Take ownership of more challenging technical tasks, troubleshoot issues independently before asking for support, and apply feedback from my first term to improve my solutions.

Measures of success: Complete technical tasks with less supervision, develop effective solutions to real project problems, and receive positive feedback on my problem-solving skills.

Reflection: Creating non-production and production environments and setting up OpenShift gave me opportunities to work through a different set of problems from the application logic I focused on in the winter. I made progress toward this goal by building on my knowledge of the audit automation project and considering how the application and its environment fit together. This experience strengthened the troubleshooting approach I can bring to future technical roles.

Literacy ? Technological literacy

Goal: Expand my practical knowledge of cloud technologies and development tools used by the team.

Action plan: Work with additional cloud services, automation tools, and CI/CD processes; better understand how the organization's systems connect and operate; and apply new technologies directly in project work.

Measures of success: Work confidently with more cloud tools and services, understand the architecture behind the systems I support, and apply new technologies successfully in my work.

Reflection: My work with OpenShift and environment setup broadened my practical understanding of the DevOps side of application delivery. I gained more experience with the tools and tasks involved in supporting a software project beyond writing its code. Jira administrator work also exposed me to another part of the team's tooling. These tasks helped me connect application development with the environments and processes around it.

Personal learning goal ? Software development & cloud services

Goal: Gain more hands-on experience with software development projects while building a deeper understanding of cloud services and technologies.

Action plan: Contribute to software development projects that use Azure, gain experience with services such as Azure Functions, App Services, Storage Accounts, Key Vault, and Azure DevOps, and learn how applications are deployed, monitored, and integrated within Azure.

Measures of success: Contribute to a software project using Azure services, become more confident working with Azure tools and environments, and better understand how Azure services work together in real applications.

Reflection: Continuing the audit automation project gave me the opportunity to build on the Python, FastAPI, and Azure foundation from my first term. In the summer, my focus shifted toward OpenShift and creating the application's non-production and production environments. This supported my broader goal of understanding how software is delivered, while deeper experience with the full set of Azure services in my original plan remains an area for continued learning.

04

Job description

During my first four months, I mainly worked on the audit automation project: gathering details, helping build the logic, and building the website. That work gave me a foundation in the application's purpose and how its different pieces supported audit workflows.

In my second term, I focused much more on the production aspect of the project. I worked on creating the non-production and production environments and setting up OpenShift. The emphasis moved from what the application does to how it runs within the organization's infrastructure.

This was an interesting extension of the same project because it gave me experience with another part of the software lifecycle. Working with separate environments helped me understand the importance of having a place to prepare and validate changes before they reach production. It also encouraged me to think more carefully about configuration, deployment, and the relationship between application code and its environment.

Alongside the project, I gained more experience with DevOps-related tasks, performed some Jira administrator work, and had opportunities to give presentations. These responsibilities gave me practice both working with technical systems and explaining my work to others.

The role drew on the programming and problem-solving foundation I developed in class, while the practical details of OpenShift, enterprise environments, and administrative workflows were learned on the job. Continuing for a second term helped me connect these skills and develop a broader understanding of cloud engineering.

05

Conclusions

My second term at Co-operators expanded my experience from developing the audit automation application's logic and website to working on the environments that support it. Creating non-production and production environments and setting up OpenShift helped me understand more of the connection between software development and DevOps.

Across the eight-month placement, I gained a broader view of a software project's lifecycle. The summer term also gave me valuable experience with Jira administration and presentations. I will carry this combination of technical problem solving, cloud knowledge, and communication practice into my next work experience.
